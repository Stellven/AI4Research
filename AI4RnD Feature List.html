2 Ingestion Entry: Unstructured user inputs, including a natural-language request and user-supplied materials such as PDF, Markdown, office documents, datasets, code repositories, URLs, or pasted content. A source-linked raw requirement inventory, where each raw requirement points back to the user's natural-language request or supplied input material that expressed it. 1. Request Capture Receives the user’s question, instruction, desired outcome, and requested deliverable as an intake candidate without prematurely treating it as a formal requirement.
3 2. Qualified Channel Signal Intake Accepts a V-05-qualified Slack or internal-tool clue with its permitted context, sensitivity, relevance, and provenance; raw channel traffic remains outside Workflow.
4 3. User-Supplied Material Import Imports documents, uploads, pasted content, datasets, and explicit file, repository, or URL references supplied by the user; related-source discovery belongs to Search & Ideation.
5 4. Intake Context Binding Binds the candidate to the authorized user, session, workspace, project, originating signal, and explicitly selected prior artifacts needed for interpretation.
6 5. Real-Time Intake Deduplication & Cleaning Canonicalizes incoming candidates, filters malformed or noisy content, and merges replays, exact duplicates, and near-duplicates while preserving meaningful revisions and independent corroboration.
7 6. Intake Provenance Registration Records origin, identity, version, timestamp, access path, acquisition mode, and transformations for every accepted intake input.
8 7. Intake Qualification Checks accessibility, authorization, readability, and minimum completeness, then emits a qualified intake package or an explicit rejection or quarantine reason.
9 Requirement compilation Entry: A list of raw requirements. A confirmed research and ideation brief containing interpreted objectives, scope, constraints, priorities, acceptance criteria, evidence requirements, and permitted ideation directions. 1. Intent Interpretation Determines the user’s actual problem, desired change, audience, decision, and deliverable without selecting an opportunity or solution.
10 2. Context Scoping Resolves applicable context, affected entities, time horizon, inclusions, exclusions, and the boundary of the decision to support.
11 3. Ambiguity Resolution Finds missing information, conflicting instructions, undefined terms, and consequential assumptions and resolves them through focused questions or explicit defaults.
12 4. Constraint Resolution Identifies and reconciles authorization, scope, time, cost, data, safety, policy, tool, environment, and output-format constraints with the user’s intended outcome.
13 5. Requirement Prioritization Separates mandatory outcomes, preferences, tradeable qualities, dependencies, exclusions, and deferred requests.
14 6. Acceptance Definition Defines observable success criteria, proof obligations, decision thresholds, failure conditions, and stopping rules.
15 7. Requirement Contract Confirmation Assembles a versioned contract with executable task semantics, presents consequential interpretations, and records user confirmation or authorized assumptions.
16 Search & ideation Entry: The permitted ideation directions in the confirmed research and ideation brief. An evidence-grounded candidate idea set, with source-linked supporting and opposing observations, claims, data, methods, results, technical signals, coverage, and known limitations. 1. Search Strategy Formation Turns the requirement contract into technical themes, source families, queries, time horizons, counter-signals, diversity goals, and coverage targets.
17 2. Multi-Source Signal Discovery Uses parallel research agents to search open-source projects, papers, patents, standards, experts, think tanks, industry reports, big-tech dynamics, technical communities, the open web, and authorized internal history.
18 3. Source Qualification Screens discovered material for intent relevance, authority, recency, independence, incentives, duplication, access limits, and potential bias.
19 4. Technical Signal Extraction Extracts claims, data, methods, mechanisms, results, benchmarks, limitations, failures, dependencies, adoption signals, and unresolved questions.
20 5. Signal Organization Normalizes signals, binds provenance, clusters related findings, and maps convergence, divergence, dependencies, and opposing viewpoints.
21 6. Trend & Gap Analysis Detects momentum, decay, contradictions, bottlenecks, missing capabilities, abandoned directions, and technical white spaces.
22 7. Idea Generation Expands a diverse candidate set from signal clusters, gaps, adjacencies, cross-domain transfers, and contrarian combinations while retaining initial supporting and opposing evidence.
23 8. Search Coverage Review Tests source-family, technical, geographic, temporal, viewpoint, and counter-evidence coverage before the candidate space is allowed to narrow.
24 Idea identification / screening / opportunity selection Entry: The evidence-grounded candidate idea set. A list of opportunity cards, each a structured record containing one opportunity and its relevant information, formed by synthesizing, screening, and consolidating related ideas. 1. Candidate Consolidation Combines parallel search outputs, resolves semantic duplicates, and preserves variants whose assumptions, evidence, or opportunity boundaries materially differ.
25 2. Idea Identification Converts meaningful signal combinations and gaps into discrete candidate ideas with a recognizable technical or user value proposition.
26 3. Idea Card Formation Forms the governing Idea Card with the candidate’s opportunity, relevance, linked evidence, assumptions, novelty, uncertainty, risks, and open questions; evidence formation is part of this card.
27 4. Opportunity Definition States the unmet need, technical bottleneck, missing capability, unserved user, or unexploited combination that makes the idea actionable.
28 5. Technical Opportunity Screening Screens novelty, evidence maturity, reachable data, known mechanisms, technical feasibility, and the existence of a credible verification path.
29 6. Strategic Opportunity Screening Screens user or company value, timing, defensibility, strategic fit, adoption, safety, legal exposure, dependencies, and resource implications.
30 7. Opportunity Portfolio Prioritization Ranks and selects a bounded, diverse opportunity portfolio using transparent criteria and records why other directions were deferred or rejected.
31 Generate technical claims & hypothesis Entry: The list of opportunity cards. Testable technical claims, one or more competing or complementary hypotheses, and a verification-ready POC design defining metrics, evidence requirements, success and failure criteria, constraints, and required resources. 1. Research Question & Technical Claim Formation turn passed opportunity to specific research questions and testable technical claims, clarify research object, expected effects and applicable situations.
32 2. Claim, Evidence, Data & Method Modeling Set up claim, data, means of measuring, approaches, and the relationship between baseline and missing evidence
33 3. Hypothesis Pool & Mechanism Formation Form the hypothesis from opportunities, and form the mechanism to test the hypothesis.
34 4. Falsifiability Screening & Hypothesis Contracting Determine if the hypothesis can be overthrowed by obtained data, or methods for the experiment. Also form the contract for testing the hypothesis
35 5. Verification-Ready POC Design Design the minimum viable verification plan. Define input, output, evidence requirements, index requirement for success, success/fail criteria, resource needed, restraints and verification steps.
36 POC implementation Entry: The verification-ready POC design. An implemented and testable POC artifact, in a domain-appropriate form, that realizes the POC design and is ready for benchmarking. 1. POC Implementation Environment Preparation Setup the environment needed for the POC (dependencies, data, runtime, access permissions, compute resources and configs)
37 2. POC Construction Construct the code, model, prompt, etc. for validating the POC.
38 3. POC Component Integration & Configuration Connect each part into a POC workable E2E, and prepare for the smoke test.
39 4. POC Functional Readiness Validation Perform smoke test run, make sure POC runs, outputs are in the right format, and faliures can be observed.
40 5. Testable POC Artifact Consolidation & Benchmark Handoff Organize final POC, config, dependencies, runtime, known constraints and necessary explanations, form an artifact that is benchmark-ready.
41 Benchmarking Entry: The implemented and testable POC artifact produced by POC implementation. A benchmark result containing the measured outcomes, comparison context, run conditions, and known limitations. 1. Benchmark Framing Define the claims to be compared, the runtime for the benchmark, baseline used, reference system, and version history.
42 2. Benchmark Protocol & Asset Preparation define workload, dataset, figures, repetitions, resource limits, methods for data collection, comparision rules, and reletive assets.
43 3. Benchmark Execution Run the benchmark according to the rules.
44 4. Metrics & Run Evidence Collection Collect benchmark results (index, log, run condition, status, faliure lgos, resource used, and provenance, to make sure result is trackable)
45 5. Comparative Result Analysis & Benchmark Result Packaging Compare POC and baseline, organize info and form benchmark result.
46 Evaluation 1. Evaluation Scope & Evidence Assembly Clarify the strategy and standard used for this evaluation, collect benchmark results, claims, and reletive evidence
47 2. Evidence Completeness & Provenance Review Check if evidence is complete, source tracable, completeness on key experiments, and existence of false materials
48 3. Experimental, Reasoning & External Validity Review Check experiment design, and if the result make sense in external world
49 4. Claim & Acceptance-Criteria Comparison Compare the benchmakr with technical claims, hypothesis and the original accept criteria
50 5. Verdict, Blocker & Residual-Risk Classification Tag the hypothesis as pass/fail/inconclusive/conditionally acceptable/etc.
51 6. Refinement & Follow-Up Recording Note down the points that needs improvement, but no improvements are implemented in this step.
52 Delivery 1. Delivery Planning & Evidence Handoff Define the target audience, delivery format, delivery content, evidence index, permission, and a handoff checklist
53 2. User-Facing Deliverable Generation generate the report the user needs, including proposal, technical roadmap, investment recommendation, paper, poster, rebuttal or visualization, etc.
54 3. Deliverable, Reusable Asset & Knowledge Packaging Organize all the deliverables.
55 4. Authorized Distribution, Knowledge Transfer & Lifecycle Closure Send the deliverable to the user, confirme acceptance, complete artifact transferring, save success/faliure experience, and close the current workflow lifecycle.
